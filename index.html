Drilling Methods

Specialized techniques selected based on your site's unique geological requirements for optimal results.

Hard Rock Specialty
Down-The-Hole

Precision Hard Rock Drilling

DTH (Down-The-Hole) drilling uses a pneumatic hammer at the bottom of the drill string to crush hard rock formations. The air-powered percussion provides exceptional penetration rates in granite, basalt, and other hard geological formations.

High Penetration Rate Minimal Deviation Clean Boreholes
  • Ideal for igneous and metamorphic rocks
  • Excellent sample recovery
  • Fast drilling in consolidated formations
Cased Borehole
Unstable Formations
ODEX System

Drilling in Unstable Ground

The ODEX system installs casing simultaneously as drilling progresses, preventing borehole collapse in loose or unconsolidated formations. This method is essential for drilling through gravel, sand, and fractured rock zones.

Simultaneous Casing Collapse Prevention Versatile Use
  • Perfect for overburden and alluvial zones
  • Prevents contamination between aquifers
  • Secure foundation for deeper drilling
500m+ Max Depth
Soft Formations
Mud Rotary

Deep Soft Formation Drilling

Mud rotary drilling uses a rotating drill bit with continuous circulation of drilling fluid (mud) to stabilize the borehole and transport cuttings to the surface. Ideal for clay, shale, and water-sensitive geological zones.

Deep Penetration Fluid Circulation Formation Stability
  • Excellent for sedimentary formations
  • Continuous sample analysis
  • Controls water influx during drilling
